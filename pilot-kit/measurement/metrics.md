# What to measure, and the gate thresholds

Log as you go. The three headline numbers are what the Phase-1 gate turns on; the rest give
context.

## Headline (the gate turns on these)
- **Median review minutes per submission** — from `review-log.csv`. This is the number the
  whole business model hinges on. Track the median and the spread, and whether it falls as
  reviewers get faster.
- **Top stuck points** — from the observer notes and the `needed_final_hint` column. Which
  3–5 units/concepts stalled people, and whether hints or coworkers resolved them.
- **Completion / retention** — share of learners who finished each unit and the milestone.

## Context
- **Time-to-first-review** — did reviews keep up with the SLA, or did learners wait?
- **Exit survey** (1–5): realism, difficulty calibration, confidence gained, would-recommend.
- **Qualitative** — two or three quotes per learner on what helped and what frustrated.

## Rough thresholds to interpret against (not pass/fail — judgment calls)
These are starting reference points; set your own after you see the data.
- Review minutes per submission: if the median is small enough that a sponsor could fund it
  at a realistic cohort size, the model is plausible. If it's very high, the review model
  needs rethinking (more AI-assisted first pass, milestone-only human review).
- Completion: a healthy slice holds most of the cohort through the milestone. Heavy mid-course
  drop-off points at difficulty calibration or unclear next steps — diagnose which.
- Realism/confidence: these are what make learners stay and what employers are paying for.
  Low scores here matter even if completion is fine.

## The call at the end
Write one page: the three numbers, the top stuck points with fixes, and a clear
recommendation — proceed to Phase 2, iterate and re-pilot, or stop and rethink.
